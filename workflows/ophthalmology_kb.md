## Ocular Emergencies

### Types of Ocular Emergencies
- Physical Injuries
- Chemical Injuries
- Infections
- Painless Sudden Loss of Vision
- Acute Glaucoma
- Uveitis
- Painful CN III Palsy

### Signs by Emergency Type

**Physical Injuries:** Red eye (conjunctival hyperaemia), subconjunctival haemorrhage, corneal surface irregularities/opacities, foreign body, hyphaema (blood in anterior chamber), irregular pupil, eyelid and eye laceration, periorbital haematoma.

**Chemical Injury:** Conjunctival hyperaemia, corneal opacity, corneal oedema.

**Infection:** Hyperaemia, hypopyon (pus in anterior chamber).

**Acute Glaucoma:** Corneal oedema, severe pain with headache and nausea/vomiting, fixed mid-dilated pupil (large oval).

**Uveitis:** Hyperaemia, dull headache, photophobia, smaller or same-sized pupil.

### Differential Diagnosis — Red Eye

| Feature | Conjunctivitis | Iritis | Acute Glaucoma | Keratitis/FB Abrasion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discharge | MARKED | None | None | Slight or none |
| Photophobia | None | MARKED | Slight | Slight |
| Pain | None | Slight to marked | MARKED | MARKED |
| Visual Acuity | Normal | Reduced | Reduced | Varies with lesion site |
| Pupil | Normal | SMALLER or same | LARGE OVAL and FIXED | Same or SMALLER |

## Triage Categories (SNEC)

### Category 1 — Assessment and treatment within 10 minutes
- Chemical burns (acid or alkali): Start irrigation IMMEDIATELY
- Penetrating eye injury (PEI): Apply shield if appropriate; do not touch if self-evident
- Sudden vision loss — Central Retinal Artery Occlusion (CRAO), VA <6/60
- Severe eye pain — possible acute angle-closure glaucoma

### Category 2 — Within 30 minutes (potential adverse outcome or severe distress)
- Painless loss of vision — Central Retinal Vein Occlusion (CRVO)
- History indicating penetrating eye injury (PEI)
- Hypopyon — pus in anterior chamber
- Hyphaema (total) — blood filling anterior chamber

### Category 3 — Within 60 minutes (potential adverse outcome or severe discomfort)
- Foreign body (non-penetrating)
- Painful red eye
- Flash burn (welder flash) — pain often delayed
- Sudden increase in number of floaters, especially with previous retinal history — suspect retinal detachment
- Flashes, especially with previous retinal history — suspect retinal detachment
- Small hyphaema

### Category 4 — Within 2 hours (chronic or minor condition; delay will not affect outcome)
- Conjunctivitis
- Blepharitis
- Chalazion
- Dry eyes
- Long-term floaters (no previous retinal history)
- Subtarsal foreign bodies with no eye redness

### SNEC Triage Form Key Sections
- **Section A**: Urgent conditions checklist — recent surgery/laser/injection, only seeing eye, contact lens user, history of uveitis, previous retinal detachment
- **Main symptoms**: Foreign body, herpes zoster, blunt injury, chemical injury, eyelid redness/laceration, floaters/flashes, painful eye, sudden blurring/visual field loss
- **Section B**: Outcome of triage — same-day appointment, A&E referral, early appointment
- **Section C**: Impression/diagnosis — uveitis/infective, cornea, vitreo-retinal, glaucoma, neuro-ophthal, oculoplastics, trauma, common conditions

## History Taking

### Two Scenarios
1. **Non-emergency consultation** — scheduled appointment at OPD
2. **Emergency consultation** — Acute Care Clinic or A&E

### General Health History
- Systemic conditions (especially vascular: diabetes, hypertension; inflammatory: arthritis, uveitis)
- Medications: anti-coagulants, herbal supplements, vitamins, anti-malarials, steroids
- Allergies (including eye drop allergies)
- Tetanus immunisation status (if eye trauma)
- Headaches — history of migraine, visual auras
- Recent overseas travel (if purulent discharge — acquired infection or poor hygiene)

### Ocular History — Presenting Problem (First Series of Questions)
- **Visual acuity**: Was the change sudden or gradual? Partial or total loss? One or both eyes?
- Current form of correction (glasses/contact lenses)
- Is the presenting problem contact lens related?
- Blurry or double vision
- Sudden drop in visual acuity
- Flashes and floaters / headaches
- Recent eye surgery or trauma
- Red / sore / itchy eye

### Ocular History — Past Ocular History
- Pre-injury vision (was eye always reduced — amblyopia/lazy eye?)
- Previous ocular surgery — risk of infection, wound dehiscence, IOL displacement
- Previous ocular trauma (airbag-related: flashes/floaters may appear months later)
- Use of eye drops — how often, when last used, how long bottle has been open
- Conditions affecting the other eye
- Previous contact lens use — check for overwear (using daily lenses 2–3 days), incorrect solution
- Corrective/refractive surgery history (when was last follow-up; be aware of overseas surgery)

### Pain Assessment
- Document pain level and type — use pain scale (0–10)
- Ask exact type and location of pain
- Analgesia taken — was it effective?
- Photophobia?
- Deep pain in and around eye
- Severe pain + nausea and vomiting → consider acute angle-closure glaucoma

### Family History
- Cataracts, Glaucoma, Retinal/Corneal Dystrophies, Retinal Detachment, Squint/Strabismus

### Summary of History Taking Framework
- **Vision**: working vision, reading vision, TV viewing
- **Ocular History**: e.g., myopia → risk of retinal detachment
- **Medical History**: e.g., diabetes → vitreous haemorrhages
- **Family History**: glaucoma, squint/strabismus
- **Medication History**: steroids, anti-coagulants, herbal supplements
- **Special Questions**: mechanism of injury, foreign body, nature of discharge

## Basic Eye Evaluation

### Visual Acuity (VA) — Distant Vision

**Definition:** The smallest object or letter that can be clearly seen and distinguished at a specified distance.

**Purpose:** Monitor distance visual status; baseline observation; part of comprehensive eye evaluation.

**When:** ALL patients, ALL visits.

**Equipment:** M&S System (Modified LogMAR) or Snellen Chart projector; occluder; alcohol wipes. Recorded in SCM (System Configuration Management).

**Charts Used at SNEC:**

*Modified LogMAR (M&S System):*
- Logarithm of the Minimum Angle of Resolution
- Inverted triangle shape; all lines show 5 letters/numbers except 6/120
- Calibrated to room distance (check working distance shown on screen)
- If machine is moved, recalibrate to new room length
- Documentation: additional letters read scored as +1/+2/+3/+4 only

*Snellen Chart / Projector:*
- Rectangular format; 11 lines of block letters
- Standard testing distance: 6 metres
- Documentation: +1/+2 or -1/-2

*E Chart:* For patients unable to read letters — young children, illiterates, non-English speakers.

**Normal VA:** 6/6 (Snellen). Fraction = testing distance / distance at which normal eye can read the letters.

**Procedure — Distance Vision with Modified LogMAR:**
1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Wipe occluder with alcohol wipes
3. Position patient comfortably facing chart
4. Check with corrective lenses or contact lenses worn
5. Test RIGHT eye first (by convention) — occlude left eye
6. Ask patient to read ALL 5 letters/numbers from left to right, starting from the top (largest) line
7. Patient must read ALL 5 before proceeding to next line down
8. Stop when patient cannot read ALL 5 letters from a line
9. Document the last completed line
10. Repeat for left eye (occlude right)
11. If vision is 6/12 or above: apply pinhole to attempt vision improvement
12. Wipe occluder with alcohol wipes; perform hand hygiene

**Pinhole Test:** A pinhole occluder eliminates peripheral rays of light. Used to determine if reduced vision is due to refractive error. If vision improves with pinhole → refractive cause. If no improvement → other cause (media opacity, retinal/optic nerve disease).

**Special Scenarios — Modified LogMAR:**
- *Scenario 1*: Patient reads partial 6/60 line → use pinhole immediately; if still cannot read all 5 → stop and record
- *Scenario 2*: Patient reads partial 6/60 → pinhole → can read whole line → test one line down with pinhole → stop when cannot read all 5
- *Scenario 3*: Cannot read 6/60 at all → proceed to 6/120 (no pinhole) → if still unable, use pinhole on 6/120
- *Scenario 4*: Cannot read 6/120 even with pinhole → proceed in order: CF (Count Fingers @ 0.5m/1m/1.5m/2m) → HM (Hand Movement) → PL (Perception of Light) → NPL (No Perception of Light)

**Key Principles (Both Charts):**
- Always start with RIGHT eye (by convention)
- Use pinhole if vision is 6/12 or above
- Aim for optimal vision with correction in place
- Highlight to doctor if vision drops from previous visit (e.g., from 6/12 to 6/120)
- VA testing provides an estimate — cannot replace formal refraction

### Visual Acuity — Near Vision

**When:** All patients on first visit and when ordered.

**Equipment:** Near Vision Chart (N chart), occluder, alcohol wipes. Room with adequate lighting.

**Procedure:** Chart held at 35 cm from patient's eyes. Record with correction in place. Test each eye separately with other eye occluded. Record smallest line patient can read comfortably.

**Normal near vision:** N5 (finest print visible). Results documented as N5, N6, N8, N10, N12, N18, N24, N36, N48.

### Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Measurement — Non-Contact Tonometry (NCT)

**When:** ALL patients, ALL visits.

**Equipment:** Non-contact air puff tonometer; alcohol wipes. Documented in SCM.

**What is IOP:** Pressure inside the eye. Fluctuates throughout the day. Normal range: 10–21 mmHg.

**NCT (Air Puff) Principle:**
1. Works on time-interval principle
2. Measures time from air puff generation to exact corneal flattening (applanation)
3. Time taken correlates with IOP
4. Less time = softer eye (lower IOP); more time = harder eye (higher IOP)
5. Air puff tonometer tends to overestimate at higher IOP values

**Procedure:**
1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Wipe all machine parts that contact patient with alcohol wipes before start
3. Request patient to remove glasses or contact lenses
4. Position patient comfortably at machine
5. Inform patient a puff of air will be blown into each eye
6. Perform NCT (right eye first by convention)
7. Wipe machine contact parts with alcohol wipes after procedure
8. Perform hand hygiene
9. Document RE and LE readings in mmHg in SCM

### Auto-Refraction (AR)

**When:** New patients; when ordered.

**Purpose:** Objective measurement of refractive error (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism). Used for new patients and pre-operative assessment.

### Auto-Keratometry (AK)

**Purpose:** Measures corneal curvature and corneal astigmatism. Used for new patients and pre-op assessment (especially for IOL power calculation in cataract surgery).

**Procedure for AR/AK (same machine):**
1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Wipe machine contact parts with alcohol wipes
3. Request patient to remove glasses or contact lenses
4. Position patient comfortably at machine
5. Inform patient to open both eyes wide and look at fixation target
6. Perform AR/AK as per doctor's order
7. Wipe machine parts with alcohol wipes
8. Perform hand hygiene

### Colour Vision — Ishihara Chart
Tests for colour vision deficiency (red-green colour blindness). Plates of coloured dots — patient identifies numbers within dots. Documented as number of plates read correctly.

### Amsler Grid Chart
Detects central visual field defects and metamorphopsia (distortion). Used to monitor macular conditions (e.g., AMD). Patient covers one eye, looks at central dot, reports any distortion, missing areas, or wavy lines. Abnormal = suspect macular disease.

## OPD Clinical Skills

### Patient Assessment — Fall Risk (Skill 2)
- Assess ALL patients for fall risk at each visit
- If YES: document action taken (e.g., accompany patient, ensure wheelchair)
- High-risk patients: elderly, post-dilation (blurred vision), visually impaired

### Instillation of Eye Drops (Skill 6)
- Document: diagnosis, purpose, eye drop name and strength
- Confirm correct eye (RE/LE/Both)
- Check patient is not allergic
- Explain procedure to patient
- Tilt patient's head back, gently pull lower lid down, instil one drop in lower fornix
- Ask patient to close eye gently and apply gentle pressure to nasolacrimal area

### Pupil Dilation (Skill 6a)
- Document: eye to dilate (both/right/left), pre-dilation pupil size in mm (RE and LE), dosage (number of instillations), time taken
- Common dilating drops: tropicamide 1%, phenylephrine 2.5%
- Warn patient: blurred near vision and photosensitivity after dilation (lasts 4–6 hours)
- Contraindication: narrow angles (discuss with nurse/doctor before dilating)

### Pre-Operative Assessment (Skill 11)
- Document: diagnosis, type of surgery planned
- Results to record: ECG, Blood Pressure, Blood Sugar (*if tested), Urine Sugar (*if tested)
- Document action taken for any abnormal results
- **Pre-op Counselling (Skill 11a):** Date of surgery, current medications patient is on, fasting requirement (time to fast from)

### Post-Operative Eye Dressing (Skill 13)
- Document: diagnosis, surgery performed, eye (right/left)
- For cataract surgery: record vision pre-surgery and post-surgery (with/without visual aid)
- **Observations during dressing:**
  - *Lids*: Oedema / redness / discharge / normal
  - *Conjunctiva*: redness / chemosis / normal
  - *Wound section*: haematoma / loose sutures / infection / normal
- Report any abnormal findings immediately to nurse-in-charge

### Triaging (Skill 14)
Triage is the assessment of patients to determine clinical priority.

**Documentation per patient:**
- Chief complaint
- Signs/symptoms related to chief complaint
- Anatomical parts involved
- Provisional diagnosis (by student OA/OT)
- Actual diagnosis (as written in clinical notes — for learning comparison)
- Outcome: earlier TCU / Acute Care Clinic / Keep to current appointment
- Patient education given (if patient not proceeded for consultation)

### Assisting in Consultation Room (Skill 15)
- Document: NP (new patient) or Follow-up
- Dilation: DBE / RE / LE / Nil
- Investigations ordered for diagnosing
- Diagnosis, findings to support diagnosis
- Management plan, TCU (next follow-up), medications ordered

### Lensometry (Skill 8)
- Measures the power of spectacle lenses
- Document: diagnosis, purpose of test
- Used to verify patient's current spectacle prescription

### Retinoscopy (Skill 9)
- Objective measurement of refractive error using retinoscope
- Document: diagnosis, purpose

### Lid Hygiene (Skill 10)
- Procedure for blepharitis management
- Document: diagnosis, purpose, whether procedure was observed (Yes/No)

## Ophthalmic Investigations Skills

### A-Scan Biometry (Skill 1 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Measures axial length (length of the eyeball)
- **Indication:** Pre-cataract surgery IOL (intraocular lens) power calculation
- **Method:** Contact or immersion technique; ultrasound
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, RE/LE/Both, preparation, axial length findings
- Preparation includes: explaining procedure, instilling topical anaesthetic if contact method

### Optical Coherence Biometry (Skill 2 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Non-contact measurement of ocular biometry (axial length, corneal curvature, anterior chamber depth)
- **Advantage over A-scan:** Non-contact; more accurate for IOL calculation in most eyes
- **Indication:** Pre-cataract surgery, contact lens fitting
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, RE/LE/Both, findings

### Endothelial Cell Count (Skill 3 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Measures corneal endothelial cell density (cells/mm²)
- **Normal:** >2000 cells/mm²; concern raised if <1500 cells/mm²
- **Indication:** Pre-cataract surgery, Fuchs endothelial dystrophy, contact lens wearers, post-corneal transplant
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, RE/LE/Both, findings (cell density, morphology)

### Flare Eye Test (Skill 4 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Measures aqueous flare (protein concentration in anterior chamber) — indicator of intraocular inflammation
- **Indication:** Uveitis monitoring, post-surgical inflammation assessment
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, RE/LE/Both, findings (flare value)

### Heidelberg Retinal Tomography — HRT (Skill 5 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** 3D laser scanning of optic nerve head; measures retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) thickness and optic disc parameters
- **Indication:** Glaucoma diagnosis and monitoring
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, RE/LE/Both, findings (optic disc classification, RNFL data)

### Anterior Segment OCT — ASOCT (Skill 6 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** High-resolution cross-sectional imaging of anterior segment (cornea, anterior chamber, angle, iris, lens)
- **Indication:** Corneal disease assessment, angle assessment (glaucoma), post-surgical monitoring, refractive surgery planning
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, RE/LE/Both, findings

### Humphrey Visual Field — HVF (Skill 7 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Automated static perimetry — maps peripheral and central visual field sensitivity
- **Standard tests:** 24-2 or 30-2 programme (most common for glaucoma); SITA Standard or SITA Fast
- **Indication:** Glaucoma monitoring, neurological conditions, retinal disease
- **Key reliability indices:** Fixation losses, false positives, false negatives (high values = unreliable test)
- **Pattern of loss:**
  - Arcuate/Bjerrum scotoma → glaucoma
  - Altitudinal defect → ischaemic optic neuropathy
  - Hemianopia → neurological (chiasm/optic tract lesion)
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, type of test programme, interpretation of results, reliability indices

### Goldman Visual Field — GVF (Skill 8 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Manual kinetic perimetry — maps isopters (lines of equal sensitivity) across visual field
- **Advantage:** Can test very large visual fields; useful for patients who cannot cooperate with automated testing
- **Indication:** Advanced glaucoma, neurological conditions, low vision assessment, disability certification
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, interpretation of results

### Potential Acuity Meter — PAM (Skill 9 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Predicts potential visual acuity after cataract surgery by projecting a Snellen chart directly onto the retina, bypassing the opacified lens
- **Indication:** Pre-cataract surgery in patients with dense cataract, to assess retinal function
- **Preparation:** Pupil dilation required
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, preparation, interpretation of results (predicted post-op VA)

### Corneal Topography (Skill 10 — Oph Inv)
- **Purpose:** Maps corneal curvature and elevation across the entire corneal surface
- **Indication:** Keratoconus screening, pre-LASIK assessment, contact lens fitting, monitoring corneal disease
- **Keratoconus pattern:** Inferior corneal steepening, asymmetric bow-tie pattern, irregular astigmatism
- **Document:** Diagnosis, indication, interpretation of results (K readings, pattern, irregularity)

## Dayward and Operating Theatre Skills

### Dayward Pre-Operative Assessment
- Use DISM mnemonic:
  - **D** — Diagnosis
  - **I** — Indication for surgery
  - **S** — Surgery planned
  - **M** — Medical conditions
- Anaesthesia type: GA (General Anaesthesia) / LA (Local Anaesthesia) / Topical
- Eye to be operated: Right / Left / Both
- Document vital signs if required

### Dayward Post-Operative Care
- Monitor and document: BP (blood pressure), RR (respiratory rate), PR (pulse rate)
- Document: TCU — time and date of next follow-up appointment
- Record post-op medications prescribed
- Observe patient until stable for discharge

### Operating Theatre Observation
- **Before surgery:** Document DISM + anaesthesia type + eye
- **Recovery area:** Document time of arrival, surgery performed, BP/RR/PR, time transferred back to dayward

## Orthoptics Assessment (Skill 17)

### Tests
1. **Versions and Ductions** — Eye movement assessment in all gaze directions
2. **Cover/Uncover Tests** — Detect manifest (tropia) or latent (phoria) strabismus
3. **Confrontational Visual Field Test** — Gross assessment of visual field using examiner's hand movements
4. **Krimsky and Hirschberg** — Measure angle of ocular deviation using light reflex and prisms
5. **Near Point Convergence (NPC)** — Distance at which convergence breaks down (normal: <10 cm)
6. **Near Point Accommodation (NPA)** — Nearest point of clear focus

### Documentation
- Assessment type, purpose, findings (RE and LE separately), interpretation of results
